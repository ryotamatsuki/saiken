# Benchmark Case Notes — What Strong Consulting Reports Actually Do

## 1. Executive Summary is not a mini table of contents

Across McKinsey, BCG, PwC, EY and Accenture, the opening section does not merely summarize what each chapter contains. It makes the central management case up front.

Typical sequence:

1. What changed in the external environment?
2. What does the evidence show?
3. Why does the current operating model no longer suffice?
4. What should leaders do differently?
5. What value or risk is at stake?

BCG Treasury Benchmarking is a particularly clear example. It opens with a small number of quantified takeaways, then moves from the changing treasury mandate to balance-sheet optimization, operating model, analytical capability and finally a short leadership agenda. The structure makes the conclusion visible before the details.

### Implication for Ehime

The current Executive Summary is already one of the strongest parts of the report. The next revision should not materially lengthen it. Instead, tighten it around four decision messages:

- why the issue matters now;
- how much liquidity Ehime structurally needs;
- what range of funds may be investable under public-data constraints;
- what operating model is required before scaling bond investment.

---

## 2. Strong reports distinguish evidence from interpretation

The better reports repeatedly signal whether a statement is:

- observed data,
- benchmark result,
- analytical interpretation,
- scenario assumption,
- recommendation.

McKinsey Global Balance Sheet explicitly avoids pretending there is a universal threshold for a healthy balance sheet. It uses long-run averages, cross-country comparisons and asset-liability relationships instead. EY survey reports disclose survey size, respondent type and geographic coverage. BCG benchmark reports identify the survey sample before generalizing from it.

### Implication for Ehime

The current report already does this relatively well when distinguishing R6 unified statistics from R7 current conditions and when refusing to infer unpublished Ehime investment balances. This discipline should be retained and made visually explicit through labels such as:

- `確認事実`
- `本調査の解釈`
- `モデル仮定`
- `愛媛県への含意`
- `提言`

---

## 3. Benchmarking is useful only when it explains a gap

Poor benchmarking says:

> Peer A = 65%, Peer B = 25%, therefore the target should be around the average.

Strong benchmarking asks:

- What is structurally different about the peer?
- What operating capability permits the observed outcome?
- Are the accounting definitions comparable?
- Does the benchmark represent performance, policy choice, or simply different constraints?

BCG and PwC survey reports use benchmark differences to expose capability gaps. EY compares leading treasurers with the broader population and asks what practices distinguish them. McKinsey compares countries but repeatedly warns against universal thresholds.

### Implication for Ehime

Each prefecture benchmark should have two columns mentally, even if not always shown in a table:

`Observed position` and `Why this may or may not transfer to Ehime`.

For the most relevant 4–6 prefectures, the revised report should create short case studies covering:

- fund size and cash/bond split;
- liquidity policy;
- maturity profile if disclosed;
- eligible instruments;
- governance structure;
- disclosure practice;
- transferability to Ehime.

---

## 4. Exhibits carry arguments, not decoration

McKinsey and BCG reports are exhibit-led. A chart normally answers a question. Its title often states the conclusion rather than the subject.

Weak title:

> R7 prefectural bond ratios

Stronger title:

> Comparable prefectures retain materially different cash buffers, indicating that bond ratio alone is not a transferable target

The body then explains why the exhibit matters.

### Implication for Ehime

Every existing figure should pass three tests:

1. What claim does this exhibit prove?
2. Would the reader understand that claim from the title alone?
3. Does the text explain the `so what`, rather than repeat the numbers?

The figures on R7 direct comparisons, Ehime liquidity demand, three scenarios, income sensitivity and maturity ladder are all important enough to remain, but their titles and interpretive text should be strengthened.

---

## 5. Strong reports use a target-state model

EY Treasury, PwC Treasury and BCG Treasury reports do not stop at describing current practice. They describe what a more mature treasury function looks like.

The target state usually combines:

- better data visibility;
- forecasting;
- integrated risk steering;
- clear governance;
- automation or repeatable processes;
- stronger analytical capability;
- regular management review.

### Implication for Ehime

The report should explicitly define a target operating model for public-fund management, for example:

**Layer 1 — Cash visibility**
13-week and monthly cash forecasts.

**Layer 2 — Fund-level ALM**
Purpose, legal floor, planned withdrawals, non-cash assets, 1–5 year pipeline.

**Layer 3 — Liquidity segmentation**
Immediate cash, near-cash, <=1y maturities, 1–3y, 3–5y, core long-term funds.

**Layer 4 — Investment execution**
Eligible assets, limits, timing, ladder, diversification.

**Layer 5 — Governance and monitoring**
Committee, quarterly review, stress testing, KPI, public disclosure.

This target state should become the backbone of Chapters 10–15.

## 6. Recommendations are conditional, not absolute

The best reports avoid false precision where uncertainty is material. They still make recommendations, but the recommendation is conditional.

A strong formulation has four components:

`Action + rationale + precondition + revision trigger`

Example for Ehime:

> Develop a standard case around roughly 435 billion yen only as an initial policy envelope, because it preserves one-year liquidity coverage for the historical maximum fund-transfer need. Do not treat it as an investment target until fund-level R7/R8 cash balances and 1–5 year spending schedules validate the amount; revise downward if those schedules show higher committed outflows.

This is stronger than either extreme:

- “435.32 billion yen is optimal”; or
- “more study is needed.”

---

## 7. Scenario analysis needs boundary conditions

Consulting reports use scenarios not only to show a range, but to show what changes between scenarios.

For Ehime, the three cases should therefore specify more than bond amount and percentage. Each case should explain:

- liquidity coverage standard;
- cash forecast accuracy assumed;
- share of spending matched by maturities;
- operational capability required;
- tolerance for market-value volatility;
- review frequency;
- conditions under which the case is inappropriate.

The current labels `保守 / 標準 / 積極` are intuitive, but the client-ready version should make the operational assumptions behind them explicit.

---

## 8. Implementation is part of the recommendation

BCG, Deloitte, Accenture and McKinsey public-sector work consistently moves from diagnosis to capability and execution.

A recommendation is incomplete if it does not answer:

- Who owns it?
- What data are required?
- How often is it updated?
- What decision forum approves changes?
- What limits trigger escalation?
- What happens in the first 90 days?

### Implication for Ehime

The current roadmap is directionally correct but should add roles and deliverables. For example:

| Period | Deliverable | Owner concept | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–90 days | Fund-level ALM register, 13-week forecast prototype | Treasury/finance working team | Validate liquidity categories |
| 3–6 months | Annual investment policy and limits | Finance/accounting governance body | Approve pilot envelope |
| 6–12 months | Ladder pilot and quarterly dashboard | Execution + oversight | Scale / hold / reduce |
| Annual | Performance and liquidity review | Senior management | Reset next-year limits |

Actual organizational titles should be inserted only after confirming Ehime's governance arrangements.

---

## 9. Strong public reports make limitations visible without weakening the message

Good reports do not hide missing data. They explain how the missing data affects the conclusion.

The current Ehime report does this well for R7 fund-level cash balances. The next step is to convert the limitation into a decision rule:

> Until R7/R8 fund-level cash balances are available, the 435.32 billion yen case is a top-down envelope rather than a validated investable amount.

Then state exactly what new data would turn the envelope into a validated figure.

---

## 10. Text volume should follow analytical importance

The benchmark does not support a rule such as “each chapter should have X pages.” Some strong reports are short; others exceed 40 pages. What is consistent is asymmetric allocation of space.

High-value sections receive more explanation:

- core decision logic;
- methodology;
- major exhibits;
- scenario assumptions;
- recommendations and implementation.

Background that does not change the decision is compressed.

### Implication for Ehime

The likely expansion priority is:

1. Chapters 9–11: liquidity, investable amount, portfolio design
2. Chapters 13–15: risk, governance, implementation
3. Chapters 4–7: peer cases and transferability
4. Chapter 12: economics and value case

Chapters 1–3 should be improved selectively, but should not become a long history of monetary policy or generic municipal finance.

---

# Synthesis: What is an excellent consulting report?

An excellent report is not defined by polish alone. It has an auditable evidence chain and a visible decision chain.

**Evidence chain**

`Source → Fact → Comparable metric → Analysis → Stress / counterargument`

**Decision chain**

`Management question → Insight → Implication → Recommendation → Implementation → Monitoring trigger`

The report is client-ready when both chains meet. A document can have perfect citations but weak decision logic, or strong recommendations but weak evidence. Neither is sufficient.

For the Ehime work, the target is therefore:

> **public-sector evidence rigor + senior consulting decision logic + treasury/ALM technical depth**

That combination should be treated as the Stage 5 quality standard.
